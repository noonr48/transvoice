# MTF Voice — Body Atlas

Comprehensive research synthesis, 5 parallel lanes, 2026-07-26/27.
Scope: **male-to-female only** (per the `mtf-only-scope-pivot` decision).
Register: **pure biology** — every instruction names a body part, a movement, and
a check. No metaphor vocabulary. No equipment. Nothing assigned as homework.

Lanes: A larynx (pitch/weight muscles) · B vocal tract (resonance) · C posture,
breath, palpation · D clinical evidence + targets · E community pedagogy decoded.
~120 sources total; per-claim citations live in the lane digests, key ones inline.

## §0 THE CUE-VALIDITY LAW (read this before writing any cue)

A sensation may be used as a check **only if it is physically caused by the
variable being trained.** Three lanes independently converged on this, and it
kills two instruction classes that feel intuitive:

**INVALID — felt vibration as a resonance signal.** Chest, face, cheekbone,
"mask" and sinus buzzing track the **pitch** being produced, not the shape of the
vocal tract. Sundberg damped singers' resonances by filling the paranasal
sinuses with liquid: voice quality did not change. Sternal accelerometry shows
chest-wall vibration is driven by **subglottal pressure oscillation** and
reflects voice-source amplitude — *"a hand on the sternum cannot confirm a
formant change."* Facial-bone vibration correlates with expert perceptual rating
at only r ≈ 0.6. A "feel it move forward" cue therefore fires whether or not
anything changed — worse than no cue.

**INVALID — naming an intrinsic laryngeal muscle and asking the learner to feel
it work.** Canonical proprioceptors are largely absent from the intrinsic
laryngeal muscles; a dedicated study found **no muscle spindles at all in
cricothyroid**, the main pitch muscle. There is no "tilt your cricothyroid"
instruction that can land. The documented failure mode is that learners overdrive
to feel *something* — the muscle-tension-dysphonia pathway. Correct production is
**nearly sensationless**; teach that as a feature, not a problem.

**VALID — the seven signals.** Everything else routes through these:

| # | Signal | Reads | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tongue sides pressed against inner faces of upper molars | Tongue-body height/frontness (resonance) | **Strongest** — direct mechanoreception, measured in >97% of running speech |
| 2 | Fastest/coolest airstream location on the palate | Where the tract's narrowest point is | Strong (mechanism) |
| 3 | Palm flat on breastbone | Vocal **weight** / pressedness — *not* resonance | Strong (accelerometry); valid below ~300 Hz, so across the whole target range |
| 4 | Fingertip on thyroid cartilage | Fold vibration is genuinely transmitted (EGG sits here); larynx **travel** during swallow/yawn | Strong for travel; height as *change* only, never absolute |
| 5 | Two fingertips in the submental triangle (under chin) | Suprahyoid load — must stay **soft** during speech | Moderate |
| 6 | Fingertip on side of nose + nostril pinch/release | Velopharyngeal port open/closed | Strong — standard clinical nasal-occlusion test |
| 7 | Effort level, and sound texture | Weight & strain. Light = "feels like any other voice"; heavy = "need to push". Harsh **buzz** = pressing; **rumble** = true weight | Strong |

**Population caveat:** fine landmark palpation on the throat is far less accurate
on feminine necks — cricothyroid-membrane identification was **69.4% on male vs
19.4% on female necks** (median error 0 mm vs 11 mm), because feminine thyroid
laminae meet at ~118.6° vs ~95.9°. **Use gross landmarks only** (the whole
cartilage, the swallow excursion) — never the cricothyroid gap.

## §1 BANNED VOCABULARY → REPLACEMENT

| Banned term | What it actually names | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| brighter / brightness | Underdetermined — four different configurations (small size, overfullness, nasality, tongue-root distortion). Retired in the field for lack of specificity | Name which one. Usually: tongue body up and forward, lip corners back |
| forward placement / the mask | Nothing moves; sound is not placed. Where legitimate, it is shorthand for a shorter tube | "Tongue body further forward, lip corners drawn back" |
| head voice / chest voice | Two claims share one name. By *felt location*: folklore. By *register* (a run of pitches sharing one fold vibration pattern): legitimate | "Full-depth fold vibration" vs "thin-edges-only vibration" |
| open throat | ≥3 separable acts, and its measurable correlate is ~8 mm larynx **lowering** + ~22% hypopharyngeal widening — a **resonance-masculinizing** change | "Stop squeezing — the throat is already open when you breathe" |
| support / sing from the diaphragm | The diaphragm is an *inhalation* muscle, not voluntarily controllable during exhalation | "Let the ribs sink slowly so air pressure stays even across the sentence" |
| big dog / small dog | Panting imitation that recruits larynx raisers/lowerers without muscle-targeting | Keep the *exercise*, drop the name: "Pant lightly and quickly — the voice box rides up under your fingers" |
| twang | Aryepiglottic sphincter narrowing (same thing as the "singer's formant") | "The ring of tissue just above the vocal folds narrows" |
| size / resonance (as felt buzz) | Vocal tract length + cross-section | "The tube from voice box to lips gets shorter" |
| weight | Fold vibratory depth + closed quotient | "How deep into the folds the vibration reaches" |
| smiling voice | Real acoustics: lip spreading shortens the tube | "Draw the lip corners back, lips flat against the teeth" |

## §2 THE QUALITIES

### 2.1 RESONANCE — the mouth-and-throat tube. **The highest-leverage axis.**

**The physics.** The tract is a tube closed at the folds, open at the lips:
Fn = (2n−1)·c/4L. Shorter tube ⇒ every formant rises by the same *percentage*.
MRI: adult male ≈ **16.9 cm**, adult female ≈ **14.1 cm** — a ~20% gap, arising
almost entirely from **pharyngeal** growth, not oral. Shift rate **≈6–7% per cm**
= +32 Hz F1 / +96 Hz F2 / +161 Hz F3 per cm at a 17 cm baseline.
(Formant-*estimated* lengths run ~1 cm shorter than MRI — **do not mix scales**.)

**Where to narrow, and why** (perturbation theory — this is what makes an
anatomical cue possible at all):
- **F2 rises** when the constriction is at its pressure maximum: the **tongue
  body raised toward the hard palate**, ~5–6 cm behind the lips.
- **F2 falls** when constriction is at a velocity maximum: **pharyngeal
  narrowing** and **lip constriction**. Generic throat-squeezing is therefore
  backwards *and* is the strain pattern.
- **F3 rises** with constriction ~3–3.5 cm behind the lips (postalveolar). In a
  front vowel F3 is a front-cavity resonance: **the entire male–female /i/ F3 gap
  is about 3 mm of front-cavity length.**
- **F1 rises** with jaw lowering — the single most effective lever on F1.
- **F3 killer:** hollow space *under* the tongue blade. MRI of American /r/ shows
  the sublingual cavity drags F3 down to **1400–2100 Hz** (a 1000–1500 Hz fall).

**What the learner does.** Tongue tip resting lightly at or just behind the lower
front teeth (not curled back). The **body** of the tongue humped up and forward,
highest point under the hard palate about a finger-width behind the ridge, with a
shallow midline groove. **Sides of the tongue in continuous contact with the
inner faces of the upper molars and premolars.** Lip corners drawn straight back,
lips flat against the teeth, never protruded. Jaw released to about a
finger-width between the back teeth, moving independently of the tongue.

**Check:** signal 1 (that side contact is a continuous band of pressure from the
last molar forward) and signal 2 (fast, cool air over a specific patch of
palate).

**Hazards.** (a) Lip **rounding** both constricts at a velocity maximum and
lengthens the tube — all formants fall; ~−6% per 1 cm of protrusion. (b) Tongue
**root** advancement widens the pharynx (raises F2) but **lowers F1 by
100–300 Hz** and usually drags the larynx down with it — the productive move is
the tongue **body**, not a root push. (c) Over-opening the jaw flattens the
tongue body away from the palate and pulls F2 back down.

**Evidence: MODERATE.** Two interventional studies moved formants *and* listener
femininity ratings (resonance-only therapy, n=10, 5 sessions: F1/F2/F3 up, **F3
significantly**; articulation exercises, n=13: F2 and F3 up, both rated
significantly more feminine). Small samples, and F0 rose concurrently.

### 2.2 PITCH — necessary, not sufficient

**Mechanism.** Cricothyroid tilts the thyroid cartilage forward-down on the
cricoid (pars recta rotates, pars obliqua translates), lengthening and thinning
the folds. Thyroarytenoid is its antagonist — it shortens and thickens. Whole-
larynx **elevation** also contributes: videofluoroscopy found vertical rise of
hyoid/thyroid/cricoid predicted F0 with adjusted R² > 0.9 each, while
cricothyroid distance alone gave 0.4.

**What the learner does.** Slide a sustained tone upward in one unbroken line
**without getting louder** — loudness recruits thyroarytenoid and re-adds weight.
Let the whole voice box travel up rather than holding it down. Habitual pitch is
a resting posture, not an event: raise the pitch each sentence *returns* to, and
the rest follows.

**Check:** signal 4 (larynx rides with the tone, not held), signal 7 (it should
stay easy — needing to push means you swapped stretching for squeezing).

**The pressure trap — and it is our engine's problem too.** F0 rises **2–6 Hz per
cmH₂O** of subglottal pressure. So a learner can reach a pitch band by *pushing
air*, producing a loud, effortful, unsustainable voice that a pitch scorer reads
as on-target. Vocal loading also raises F0 through a session, returning to
baseline only after ~5 min rest — so this drifts in exactly when fatigue sets in.
**Pitch must not be scorable as a pass in isolation.**

**Evidence: STRONG.** F0 accounts for **41.6% of the variance** in listener
gender perception (meta-analysis, 38 studies) and is the only acoustic measure
that shifts the odds of woman-attribution. **But** resynthesis work shows F0
alone usually *fails* to flip perceived sex; F0 + formants together flip it ~82%
of the time. The apparent F0-vs-resonance dispute is **design-driven, not
substantive**: correlational studies favour F0, causal resynthesis shows F0 is
necessary-but-not-sufficient.

### 2.3 VOCAL WEIGHT — the axis the chest palm actually reads

**Mechanism.** Two measurable things: **vertical thickness of the folds'
contacting surface**, and **how long per cycle they stay in contact**.
Thyroarytenoid activation from 0→100% raised medial thickness from **2.5 mm to
5.1 mm** in a male larynx model. Thicker surface ⇒ longer closed phase ⇒ more
negative H1–H2 ⇒ stronger high harmonics. *This is the mechanical reason heavier
phonation reads masculine.* Lateral cricoarytenoid is **not** interchangeable
with thyroarytenoid — it medialises the *superior* surface and actually decreases
effective thickness.

**What "lighter without breathy" means mechanically.** The resonant-voice target
is folds **barely abducted or barely adducted** — optimum modelled at ~0.6 mm
glottal half-width, where intraglottal pressure is maximal and **impact stress is
minimal**. Contact still happens every cycle; what drops is collision force.
*Less collision, not less closure.*

**Body-only ways in** (no equipment): sustained voiced **/m/ /n/ /ŋ/** (occlusion
by lips and soft palate), **lip trill**, **tongue trill**, **hand cupped over own
mouth**. A controlled trial found nasal-consonant resonant training gave
short-term quality change comparable to straw phonation. They are not
equivalent: humming and hand-over-mouth are *steady* (small swing in fold
contact); the trills are *fluctuating* (large swing, second vibrating source).

**Check:** signal 3 — buzz arrives with voice onset and stops instantly; making
the buzz **weaker at constant loudness** is the trained direction; buzz turning
**hard and rattly** means rising pressure with tight contact. Signal 7 — harsh
buzz is pressing, rumble is genuine weight.

**Do not use breathiness to sound lighter.** It does not reduce weight; it
*masks* it, becomes a habit that blocks later training, and the clinical evidence
on breathiness is mixed. Also note the task split: higher CPP in **connected
speech** reads *more* feminine, but higher CPP from a **sustained vowel** reads
*less* feminine.

**Evidence: WEAK** as a trained target — the parameters are measurable, but **no
trial trains weight as an isolated target with listener outcomes.** (Community
practitioners rate it the unlock; the literature has not tested it.)

### 2.4 NASALITY — **genuinely contested, do not coach it as a lever**

Levator veli palatini elevates and retracts the velum against the pharyngeal
wall; tensor veli palatini controls stiffness, not position. Closure also needs
palatopharyngeus and the superior constrictor.

Open-port acoustics: an added pole–zero pair near 1 kHz, an extra low pole at
250–450 Hz, zeros at 700–1800 Hz, and a **widened, damped, weakened F1**. The
port is exquisitely sensitive — a coupling area of only **0.035–0.057 cm²** is
enough to flip a listener's percept.

**Against** using it: it degrades exactly the formant structure that carries the
gender cue, and clinically it is treated as an unwanted by-product of raising the
larynx (the palate drops as the larynx rises). **For:** one controlled perceptual
study varying nasalance found nasality contributed significantly to
sex-stereotyped perception. **Unresolved.** Treat as a thing to *detect and
control*, not to add.

**Check:** signal 6 — fingertip on the side of the nose (buzz = port open), and
pinch the nostrils mid-vowel (sound changes = port was open). Note this
distinguishes real nasal coupling from mere bone-conducted facial buzz.

### 2.5 PROSODY / INTONATION

Measured as % of utterances with upward shifts and utterance semitone range;
both associate with female attribution. **No published semitone target exists** —
the evidence is directional only. One RCT (n=30, 4 weeks) raised all acoustic
intonation parameters *and* listener femininity ratings. **Evidence: MODERATE.**

Note what does **not** work: **speech rate/tempo is not associated with gender
perception** at all (it predicts *naturalness*). Don't coach rate as a femininity
lever.

### 2.6 BREATH AND PRESSURE

Subglottal pressure controls loudness (doubling it gives ~9–11 dB) **and** pitch
(2–6 Hz per cmH₂O). Steady pressure is what produces steady pitch: **sag at the
end of a sentence is decaying pressure, not laryngeal failure.**

Transversus abdominis is the abdominal muscle actually modulated with breathing,
recruited before external oblique and rectus. Trained singers set the abdominal
wall **before** peak lung volume — the pre-phonatory set. The rib cage remains
the dominant volume contributor even in classical singers (~35% abdominal vs
~14% untrained), so "belly breathing" as a rule is wrong.

**Check:** one palm on the lower side ribs (should rotate *outward* on inhale,
not lift vertically), one below the navel (should move *inward* just *before*
voice starts, not during).

**Evidence: essentially untested.** Respiratory training for voice disorders has
no clinical-trial base.

## §3 POSTURE AND PLACEMENT

- **Craniovertebral angle** ~49.9° is normal; below ~48–50° is forward head
  posture, which correlates with higher self-perceived phonatory effort
  (p<0.005), and exaggerated forward/backward head positions were worst on
  effort (p<0.001).
- **Chin down ≠ chin tuck.** A *nod* at the joint under the skull preserves hyoid
  excursion (12.1 mm); a full cervical **tuck** narrows the laryngeal inlet
  (14.0 vs 16.3 mm) and cuts hyoid travel (9.6 vs 12.6 mm). Neck flexion removes
  low-frequency energy (useful direction); neck extension adds it.
- **Shoulders are a low-yield target for conversation.** Upper trapezius is an
  accessory *expiratory* muscle — active in long/loud phrases, essentially
  inactive in simple speaking. Sternocleidomastoid and scalenes are
  inspiratory-phased; shoulder lift on inhale is them doing rib-cage work, and it
  is what produces neck fatigue.
- **Jaw–larynx coupling.** The suprahyoids run mandible → hyoid, and the larynx
  hangs from the hyoid. Restricted jaw glide raises suprahyoid load and the
  larynx rides up. Release by **movement** (slow lateral translation, teeth
  apart, ~1 cm each way; slow protrusion/retraction), not by a "relax"
  instruction, which produces no measurable change in masseter tone.
- **Effort ≠ height.** Raising vocal effort significantly raised both suprahyoid
  and infrahyoid activation yet did **not** change formant-estimated tract
  length. Pushing harder does not shorten the tube — position does.
- **Sitting vs standing** changes the lung volume you phonate at; standing adds
  downward tracheal traction on the larynx. Expect a different pressure
  requirement, not a different technique.

## §4 CONNECTED SPEECH — where it is actually won or lost

Split the variables:

- **POSTURAL** (hold constant across the whole utterance): tongue-side bracing,
  unprotruded lip corners, larynx height, velum up.
- **SEGMENTAL** (let these move): tongue tip, jaw height, tongue body front-back.

Bracing survives >97% of running speech precisely because it is postural — it is
not re-established sound by sound. Coarticulation's carryover pull is strongest
between **incompatible** gestures, so one hostile segment contaminates the vowels
on both sides of it.

**Cooperative sounds:** /i/ /ɪ/ /e/ /ɛ/, /j/, /s t d n/, clear /l/.
**Hostile:** **/r/ — the worst** (sublingual cavity drops F3 by up to 1500 Hz),
/u o ɔ/, /w/, dark /l/, /ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/ (English lip rounding).
**/ɑ/ double-fights:** good for F1, bad for F2 (pharyngeal constriction at the F2
velocity maximum), and it releases bracing **40.5%** of the time — the vowel
where the configuration collapses first.

Also: women's measured vowel spaces are **larger** (greater F1 range).
Under-articulating shrinks the space in the masculine direction independently of
tube length.

**The carryover numbers, stated plainly.** Perceived-female rates went
**1.9% before → 50.8% immediately after therapy → 33.1% at 15 months** — about a
35% relative loss without maintenance. And in the RCT, the **lower-limit f0 in
spontaneous speech did not change at all**, while reading and sustained-vowel f0
rose +49 Hz. Spontaneous speech is the documented hard part.
*(This is the exact gap a conversational coach exists to close.)*

## §5 SAFETY

**Stop the session immediately on:** pain or burning while voicing; a tone that
will not start without a hard push; needing more air pressure than a minute ago
for the same loudness; quality degrading over minutes rather than improving; new
voice breaks or tremor; a persistent lump sensation; submental firmness that does
not soften within 30 seconds of stopping.

**Muscle tension dysphonia** presents as effortful phonation, fatigue worsening
through the day, neck or anterior throat pain, loss of the top of the range,
globus, frequent throat clearing; it improves with rest and worsens with stress.
Objectively: raised hyoid and larynx during phonation, increased
sternocleidomastoid/suprahyoid/infrahyoid/cricothyroid activity.

**Refer to ENT/laryngology:** hoarseness persisting beyond 2 weeks; sooner with
pain, coughing blood, swallowing difficulty, neck mass, airway symptoms, or
smoking history. Also: prior laryngeal surgery/injection, prior intubation, or
suspected pathology. Pathologic stroboscopy was found in **5%** of 61 trans women
seeking voice care. *(The often-repeated "up to 30% of MTF voice clients had a
voice disorder" traces to a clinic blog, not a peer-reviewed series — do not
repeat it.)*

**Contraindicated practices** (the coach must never emit these):
- **Falsetto-based pitch** — thin/artificial timbre, significantly reduced range.
- **Over-elevating pitch** — documented mild-to-moderate dysphonia and tension.
- **Whisper drills / whisper sirens** — audible whispering requires false-fold
  constriction, which learners carry into voiced speech. *(Community-sourced;
  no trans-voice-specific clinical citation found — flagged.)*
- **Swallow-and-hold**, "push the larynx as high as possible" — recruits
  swallowing muscles irrelevant to speech; tension-dysphonia risk.
- **Deliberate breathiness to sound light** — masks weight, trains a dead end.
- **Pitch-only strategy** — predictably insufficient, not merely suboptimal.

**Pre-screen (feminization-specific):** pre-existing strained/strangled quality,
frequent creak in general conversation, voice tremor or breaks predict poor
outcome and unpredictable dysphonia.

**Hormones and surgery, factually.** Estrogen + anti-androgen does **not** raise
F0 after an androgenic puberty — those laryngeal changes are irreversible; after
5–10 years of hormone therapy F0 remains higher than cis men but often lower than
cis women. Wendler glottoplasty raises F0 by a mean **+78.5 Hz** (range 20–112),
but it changes **only pitch** — not resonance, prosody or articulation — and
carries short-term losses in voice quality and range with progressive recovery.

## §6 TARGET NUMBERS

**Speaking fundamental frequency**

| Band | Value |
|---|---|
| Masculine-read | < 130 Hz |
| Cis-male typical | 80–150 Hz (~125 Hz avg) |
| Ambiguous zone (formants decide) | **145–165 Hz** |
| Androgynous / gender-neutral | 145–175 Hz |
| Feminine-read | **> 180 Hz** |
| Cis-female typical | 150–250 Hz (~200 Hz avg) |
| Typical post-therapy plateau | **145–155 Hz** |

*Note: 207 Hz alone does not guarantee female attribution — resonance still
decides. And the commonly-quoted "155–165 Hz" is narrower than every published
bracket.*

**Vowel formants** (Hillenbrand et al. 1995; 45 men / 48 women)

| Vowel | Male F1/F2/F3 | Female F1/F2/F3 |
|---|---|---|
| /i/ heed | 342 / 2322 / 3000 | 437 / 2761 / 3372 |
| /æ/ had | 588 / 1952 / 2601 | 669 / 2349 / 2972 |
| /ɑ/ hod | 768 / 1333 / 2522 | 936 / 1551 / 2815 |
| /u/ who'd | 378 / 997 / 2343 | 459 / 1105 / 2735 |

Near-uniform **+17 to +22%**, consistent with the 16.9/14.1 cm length ratio.

**Realistic gains.** Therapy meta-analysis: **+27 Hz** sustained vowel, **+39 Hz**
reading, **+25 Hz** spontaneous. RCT (n=30, 14 wks): **+49/+49/+29 Hz**. Sessions
across studies ranged 5–84; real-world clinic mean **5.7 ± 3.1**. **Therapy
duration did not predict F0 gain** (p=0.63), and satisfaction was unrelated to
session count — both against the "more sessions = more change" intuition.

**Outcome measure.** TVQ-MtF: 30 items, summed 30–120, higher = worse; zones
≤54 minimal, >54–101 low, >101–140 moderate, >140 high. No formal minimal
clinically-important difference established. Reference: **81 ± 22** in 61
treatment-seeking trans women. VHI correlates only weakly with it — different
constructs.

## §7 EXERCISE BANK — all body-only, all doable in the moment

Every entry below passes the no-equipment and no-homework laws. Excluded on
equipment: straw/cup/water phonation, mirror work, pitch-monitor apps. Excluded
as homework: reference-voice mimicry, calling a friend, community feedback.

| Exercise | Trains | Instruction (biology only) |
|---|---|---|
| Light quick panting | Larynx height | "Fingers on the lump at the front of your throat. Pant lightly and quickly — feel it ride up. Now pant slow and deep — feel it slide down." Keep it **voiced**, never whispered |
| Larynx travel probe | Proprioception | "Fingertip on the lump. Yawn — it drops. Swallow — it climbs. That's the whole range" |
| Bracing hold | Resonance | "Press the sides of your tongue against the inside of your upper back teeth and keep that band of contact through a whole sentence" |
| Bracing break detection | Resonance | "Keep it, and notice it break on 'ah' and on 'l' — that's the moment the tongue drops" |
| Palate-narrow airstream | F2/F3 | "Tip behind your lower front teeth, middle of the tongue up toward the roof. Find the coolest, fastest air on the ridge behind your top teeth" |
| Lip-corner spread | All formants | "Draw the corners straight back, lips flat against the teeth. Hold it through the vowel" |
| Protrusion contrast | All formants | "Say 'oo' and feel the lips push forward — that's adding length. Now say it with corners back and lips flat" |
| /r/ awareness | F3 | "Say a word with 'r' and feel the tongue pull back and hollow underneath. Get in and out of that fast" |
| Jaw glide release | Jaw/larynx coupling | "Teeth a fingertip apart. Slide the lower jaw slowly left, then right — full smooth travel both ways" |
| Sustained /m/ /n/ /ŋ/ | Weight | "Lips closed, hold 'mmm' — steady buzz, no scratch. Then open into a vowel without changing effort" |
| Lip or tongue trill | Weight | "Blow through relaxed lips until they flutter, then add voice — the flutter must not stop" |
| Hand-over-mouth | Weight / nasality | "Cup your palm over your open mouth and speak. Strong buzz in the palm = air going out the mouth" |
| Chest-palm weight check | Weight | "Palm flat on the breastbone. Make the buzz weaker without getting quieter" |
| Soft onset | Adduction | "Start a vowel from silence so gently there's no click and no puff of air before the tone" |
| Creak → flow | Closure without pressing | "Let it drop to a slow crackle, then add the least air that turns it into a clean tone" |
| Nose-pinch test | Nasality | "Pinch your nostrils shut mid-vowel. If the sound changes, air was going up your nose" |
| Velum lift | Nasality | "Start a yawn with your mouth closed — the back roof of your mouth pulls up and back" |
| Unbroken pitch glide | Pitch | "Slide one tone upward in an unbroken line without letting it get louder" |
| Return-pitch raising | Habitual pitch | "Hold the last word of each sentence a touch longer on one note; next round let that note sit a hair higher" |
| Rib/belly hands | Breath | "One palm on the lower side ribs — it should rotate outward, not lift. Other below the navel — it moves inward just *before* voice starts" |
| Yawn-sigh | Release only | "Begin a yawn, let a sigh fall out." **Release tool only — it lowers the larynx and drops F2/F3, the wrong direction as a habit** |
| Vowel-space contrast | Mouth space | "Say 'eee' — tongue high, mouth tight. Say 'uhh' — tongue low, mouth slack. Feel the difference in space" |
| Postural carry | Carryover | "Count one to twenty keeping the tongue-side contact and flat lip corners unchanged — only the numbers change" |

## §8 CONTESTED / UNRESOLVED

1. **Nasality** — degrades formant structure (acoustics) vs contributes to
   sex-stereotyped perception (one controlled perceptual study). Unresolved.
2. **F0 vs resonance primacy** — resolved as *design-driven*: correlational
   studies favour F0 (41.6% of variance), causal resynthesis shows F0 is
   necessary but not sufficient (~82% flip only when both move).
3. **Ambiguous-zone boundaries** — 145–165 vs 145–175 vs ">180 to read feminine".
4. **Tract-length scales** — MRI vs formant-estimated disagree by ~1 cm on the
   male value. Don't mix.
5. **Aryepiglottic narrowing** — framed as efficiency-raising and
   hygiene-compatible, but the strain boundary against pharyngeal squeezing has
   **not been quantified**.
6. **Vocal weight mechanism** — open quotient vs glottal skew quotient; the
   distinction is real, the named metric is single-source.
7. **Palpation as measurement** — inter-rater r < 0.6, near-zero correlation with
   surface EMG. Valid for *self-detected change over seconds*, never for grading.
8. **Dose–response** — therapy duration did not predict gain; satisfaction
   unrelated to session count.

**Evidence-quality caveat:** every therapy trial is small (n=5 to n=74); only
three are randomised (n=30 each); the 2023 meta-analysis rated the therapy
literature low-to-moderate quality.

## §9 IMPLICATIONS FOR TRANSVOICE

1. **Pitch must not pass in isolation.** Compose the pitch verdict with the
   weight/pressedness and loudness signals — a band hit by pushing air is a false
   green, and it drifts in exactly when the learner fatigues.
2. **The cue banks need rebuilding, not editing.** 20 learner-facing strings
   currently use banned vocabulary; the replacements are §1 and §7.
3. **Add a deterministic cue law** in the shape of the existing no-homework /
   no-equipment rules: banned-metaphor lexicon + a cue-shape check (body part +
   movement + a §0 valid check).
4. **Resonance deserves more weight than it has.** It is the mechanism that
   decides the outcome inside the ambiguous zone where most learners plateau
   (145–155 Hz is the documented plateau; the zone is 145–165 Hz).
5. **The product's core bet is validated.** The literature's documented failure
   is carryover into spontaneous speech — a 35% relative loss at 15 months, and

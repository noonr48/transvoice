## SECTION 1 — SINGING vs SPEAKING (what's different for MtF)

Speaking voice uses a narrow pitch range, short phrases, one dominant register. Singing demands more of every variable: wider pitch range (1.5-2 octaves per song), sustained notes (4-8+ sec on one breath), multiple registers within one phrase, deliberate breath pacing, vibrato expected on sustained notes, expressive demands.

Feminine perception in singing differs from speaking: listeners are often MORE forgiving of a lower voice in singing (folk, jazz, indie, choral alto) and LESS forgiving in some genres (pop soprano, MT belt). Genre matters.

**When to start singing work:** AFTER speaking foundations (B7 four-layer model layers 1-3) are stable. Starting singing too early usually means pushing chest voice up, which strains and reinforces bad habits.

## SECTION 2 — THE FOUR SINGING REGISTERS (plain English)

A register is a span of notes produced by one fixed way of holding the voice box. Switching = the "break" or "passaggio."

### 2.1 CHEST VOICE — the heavy low voice
- **Sensation:** buzz strongly in the upper chest; voice box sits low; thick heavy contact; feels like speaking normally low
- **Where:** lowest ~1 octave
- **Feminine coding:** MASCULINE when used as primary singing voice. Use sparingly.

### 2.2 MIX VOICE — the balanced middle voice [THE MTF TARGET]
- **Sensation:** balanced buzz between chest and face; connected (not airy/disconnected); sustainable for long phrases; slight ring in the face
- **Where:** middle ~1-1.5 octaves — your speaking-pitch range plus some
- **Feminine coding:** FEMININE in most modern genres (pop, folk, indie, choir alto). Train this most.
- Neither "presses down" (chest) nor "floats away" (falsetto). Leans forward with weight behind it but doesn't drag.

### 2.3 HEAD VOICE — the lighter high voice
- **Sensation:** buzz moves UP into the face — front teeth, behind nose, cheekbones, behind the eyes; chest quiet; voice box rises and tips forward; feels lighter, floating
- **Where:** upper ~1-1.5 octaves
- **Feminine coding:** FEMININE in classical, choral soprano, some pop. Should CONNECT to mix below.

### 2.4 FALSETTO — the very light airy high voice
- **Sensation:** very airy; voice box tilted fully forward; folds barely touching; hooty or character-y; chest completely silent
- **Where:** same range as head voice, sometimes higher
- **Feminine coding:** CHARACTER VOICE (Minnie Mouse), NOT feminine. Useful as EXERCISE, not performance voice.

## SECTION 3 — FINDING MIX VOICE (the MtF singer's main goal)

Mix is the feminine-coded singing voice. Blend of chest (weight/connection) and head (lightness/forward buzz).

### EXERCISE 1 — The "NG" GLIDE [PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION]
Say "sing" and hold the final "ng." Feel buzz fill the back of the roof of your mouth and the space behind your nose. Glide slowly up and down on that "ng" — no breaks, smooth as a siren. The "ng" naturally places the voice box slightly lifted and the buzz forward. The range where you can sustain "ng" smoothly = your mix territory.

### EXERCISE 2 — The Siren on "OO" or "MM" [VERIFIED]
Slow siren on closed-mouth "mmm" or narrow "oo." Start in comfortable speaking range. Glide UP slowly until just before you'd "break" or flip into falsetto. Glide back down. Stay just below the break point — that's your upper mix edge. Over weeks, the edge moves up.

### EXERCISE 3 — The Small Cry [PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION]
Make the sound of a tiny sad "ow" or whimper — like a small puppy. Feel how the voice box lifts and the buzz moves forward into the face. This is the start of mix. Sustain it. Then open into a vowel ("ow" → "ah"). Stay in the cry-feeling.

### EXERCISE 4 — The Descending Slide from Head
Start in head voice on a high note. Slide DOWN slowly into middle range. As you descend, KEEP the face-buzz and the lifted feeling — don't let the chest grab back. The transition zone = mix.

### Daily mix-finding drill (10 min)
1. Sigh on "ah" ×3
2. Siren on "mmm" slow ×5
3. NG-glide ×5
4. Small cry ×5
5. Descending head-to-mix slides ×5
6. Sustain "nee" on a comfortable middle note ×3
7. Sing a single short word ("we", "no", "hi") in the mix feeling ×5

## SECTION 4 — RANGE EXPANSION (extending upper range safely)

Voice box stretch from B1 is the foundation. Applied to sustained singing:

- **5-min daily range expansion:** gentle sirens from comfortable middle up toward your top edge, NEVER pushing past the flip/strain point. Edge moves up slowly over weeks/months.
- Use "ng" or "oo" (narrow vowels make lift easier to find)
- NEVER push chest up — #1 singing injury risk
- High notes are reached by STRETCHING from below, not pushing from below

**Realistic timeline:** 2-4 semitones of upper range gain over 6-12 months of daily practice [PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION]. Beyond that requires either exceptional baseline or surgery.

## SECTION 5 — BREATH FOR SINGING (extended phrase demands)

Same foundation as B4 (low belly out, side ribs wide, stay-wide-while-speaking). For singing you scale up:
- Deeper inhale (lower belly + back ribs expand more)
- Slower, more controlled exhale (the "lean" — gentle inward+upward belly engagement)
- Per-phrase breath plan: KNOW where breaths are before performing
- Sustained notes need STEADIER breath than speech — slow controlled pour, not quick push

**Supported singing sensation:** voice feels "floated" or "leaned on" from below; throat open and free; long phrases feel possible; note feels sustainable, not fading.

---

## SECTION 6 — RESONANCE IN SINGING (moving the buzz deliberately)

In speaking, one steady posture. In singing, the buzz location MOVES:
- Lower notes: more chest buzz allowed (don't LIVE there)
- Middle notes (mix): balanced buzz chest-to-face
- High notes (head): buzz deliberately forward into face, chest quiet
- Expressive shifts: "darker" for emotional weight; "brighter" for lift

Skill = CHOOSING the buzz location per note, not stuck in one posture.

---

## SECTION 7 — VIBRATO (what it is, how it emerges)

Vibrato = slight natural wobble in pitch (~5-7 cycles/sec) that emerges WHEN voice is supported, free, balanced. NOT forced. [VERIFIED across pedagogy sources]

**How it emerges:**
- Steady supported breath (low belly lean)
- Released throat and jaw
- Balanced voice box (not pressed, not airy)
- Sustained note (takes 1-2 sec for vibrato to appear)

**Sensation when it arrives:** note feels like it starts to "spin" or "ring" — small pleasant wobble in face and throat. EASY, not effortful.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't manufacture wobble with jaw or belly pulsing (fake, looks worse)
- Don't force on every note (modern styles often use straight-tone then vibrato at end)
- Don't panic if it doesn't show up at first — months of balanced singing to emerge

For MtF: most cis female singers have vibrato, useful feminine cue. But straight-tone folk/indie also reads feminine in those genres.

## SECTION 8 — CHOIR SINGING (specific challenges)

[Sources: Chorus Connection 2022, GALA Choruses 2023, Chorus America 2017, Blurring the Binary, Reddit r/Choir]

Traditional sections: Soprano (high female), Alto (low female), Tenor (high male), Bass (low male). Binary breaks for trans singers.

**Where MtF singers typically fit:**
- Start: most pre-training MtF singers land in TENOR or BASS
- Mid-transition: many move to ALTO 2 (lowest alto), same range, different section
- Long-term: some move to Alto 1 or Soprano 2 if upper range expands. True Soprano 1 (C4-D5+) is rare without surgery or unusual baseline.

**Common choir accommodations:**
- Sing Alto part AN OCTAVE LOWER than written — fits existing range, social placement in women's section
- STAB formation (Soprano, Tenor, Alto, Bass) — separates section from gender
- Singing in gendered section (Alto/Soprano) during transition
- Open section assignment based on comfort + range, not gender

**Conversation with choir director:** have it early. LGBTQ+ chorus directors usually familiar; conservative/religious choirs harder.

## SECTION 9 — SOLO SINGING (different demands)

Solo exposes everything. Self-monitoring while performing is harder (brain on emotion/delivery, not technique).

**Repertoire for early MtF solo singers:**
- SIMPLE, LIMITED-RANGE songs (1 octave or less)
- Songs ORIGINALLY SUNG IN FEMININE REGISTERS — Tracy Chapman, Annie Lennox, kd lang, Kim Carnes (lower-voiced women) fit better than soprano pop
- TRANSPOSE to comfortable key (Chordify, MuseScore, YouTube pitch-shift)
- Gradually expand as mix strengthens
- AVOID: soprano-range pop, MT belt, anything that pushes chest-shouting

**Friendly genres:** folk, indie, jazz, alt-country, soft pop. **Harder:** soprano pop, classical soprano, MT belt.

## SECTION 10 — COMMON SINGING ERRORS FOR MTF

| Error | What it feels like | Sounds like | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pushing chest UP | Throat tightens, voice box squeezed, neck strains | Shouted, strained, "pulled chest" | Drop to mix; ng-glide or siren to find lift without push |
| Flipping to pure falsetto | Sudden airy disconnect; chest silent; character-y | Hollow, hooty, weak | Strengthen cry-feeling; descend from head into mix slowly |
| Forcing vibrato | Jaw or belly pumping; throat tension | Wobbly, fake, nervous | Stop forcing; emerges from balance, not manufacture |
| Under-supporting phrases | Voice fades mid-phrase; breath runs out | Thin, fading, pinched | Plan breaths; deeper inhale; steady lean |
| Out-of-range repertoire | Constant strain; cracks; throat hurts after | Cracked, forced, off-pitch | Transpose down; choose different repertoire |

## SECTION 11 — SINGER'S DIAGNOSTIC KIT

| Test | How | Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Siren on "mmm" | Glide slowly up and down | Voice box flexibility; smooth register transitions |
| Sustained "nee" | Hold one middle note 8 sec | Breath support; mix stability |
| "Sing" held on "ng" | Sustain the final ng | Forward buzz + throat dome coordination |
| Slow slide head-to-chest | Start high (head), slide down slowly | Mix strength (where does it grab?) |
| Comfortable song verse | Sing a verse of an easy song | Real-world integration |
| High-note soft entry | Start a high note softly (no attack) | Head voice control |
| Long phrase one breath | 8-12 syllable phrase | Breath pacing |

## SECTION 12 — 8-WEEK SINGER'S PROGRESSION (assumes speaking foundations)

**Week 1-2: Find the mix**
- Daily 10-min mix-finding drill (Section 3)
- Sustain "nee" and "ng" in mix
- No songs yet

**Week 3-4: Sustain and shape**
- Siren drills (5 min)
- Sustained mix notes (5 sec each, 10 reps)
- Simple scales on "nee" / "noh" / "ng" (5 min)
- One single sustained note per day recorded

**Week 5-6: Simple songs**
- Choose ONE simple limited-range song
- Learn it in comfortable key (transpose)
- Sing along softly daily
- Record once at end of week 6

**Week 7-8: Register transitions**
- Practice slides between mix and head (no chest grabbing)
- Add a second song with a small upper-range moment
- Daily 5-min vibrato-emergence work (sustained balanced notes)
- Record twice; self-rate posture, mix stability, breath pacing

## SECTION 13 — SURGERY WARNING FOR SINGERS

[VERIFIED — AAO-HNS Bulletin, Renee Yoxon blog, Seattle Voice Lab]

Wendler glottoplasty and other pitch-raising surgeries are usually **DISCOURAGED for singers and voice professionals**. Risks:
- Reduction or loss of singing voice range
- Reduced loudness and projection
- Loss of vocal flexibility (color, dynamics)
- Possible need for revision surgery

**A singer considering surgery should:**
1. Complete at least 12 months of voice therapy FIRST
2. Get multiple surgeon consultations
3. Specifically ask each surgeon about singing outcomes
4. Consider whether speaking-voice gain justifies singing-voice loss
5. Some trans women choose surgery for speaking gains and grieve the singing loss; some keep singing and skip surgery

This is a values decision, not a medical one.

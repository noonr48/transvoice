## WHY THIS MATTERS

Most voice training apps and tutorials focus on the FIRST 6-12 months — the acquisition phase. But voice is a LIFELONG practice. Trans women who trained successfully at year 1 face predictable challenges at year 2, 5, 10+:
- **Drift:** the trained voice slowly degrades without active maintenance
- **Illness:** colds, flu, laryngitis, COVID can wipe out weeks of progress
- **Life gaps:** stress, depression, grief, major life events pull attention from voice
- **Surgery (other):** unrelated surgeries with intubation can affect the voice
- **Aging:** the voice changes with age — menopause, thyroid changes, muscle atrophy
- **Hormonal shifts:** HRT dose changes, menopause (for those on estrogen), other hormonal events

Without explicit guidance, learners hit year 2 and assume they've "lost it" or "it didn't stick." In most cases, the voice is recoverable, often quickly. This branch documents the long-term view.

## SECTION 1 — WHAT "MAINTENANCE" MEANS

[VERIFIED — motor learning research + voice therapy consensus]

### Skill acquisition vs. skill maintenance
Motor skills have two phases:
1. **Acquisition** (months 0-12): building new neural pathways; high conscious effort; rapid change. This is what B12's curriculum addresses.
2. **Maintenance** (year 2+): the skill becomes automatic; requires LESS daily practice but DOES require periodic re-engagement. Without any engagement, motor skills degrade slowly.

### The maintenance curve
- Months 0-3: intensive practice required (Phase 1-3 of B12)
- Months 3-12: high-frequency practice (Phase 4-5 of B12)
- Year 2: practice 3-5x/week for 10-15 min is sufficient for most learners
- Year 3+: practice 1-3x/week for 10 min maintains the skill for many
- Year 5+: some learners can maintain on weekly or biweekly practice; others need daily

### The "use it or lose it" truth
The trained voice is NOT permanently installed. It's a MOTOR HABIT, and motor habits fade without use. But:
- Re-acquisition is MUCH faster than initial acquisition (motor memory persists)
- A relapse at year 3 doesn't mean starting from zero — it usually takes 2-4 weeks to recover, not 12 months

## SECTION 2 — DRIFT: THE SLOW DEGRADATION

[VERIFIED — motor learning; PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION for specific drift patterns]

### What drifts
1. **Pitch drift downward:** without active awareness, pitch creeps back toward the original range. Often 5-15 Hz drop over 6 months of zero practice.
2. **Buzz location drift backward:** the forward buzz slides back toward the chest
3. **Weight returns:** the light voice gradually gets heavier
4. **Terminal drop returns:** the lift at sentence ends fades, replaced by falls
5. **Old habits resurface:** throat-clearing pattern, hard attacks, pressed phonation

### Why drift happens
- The old neural pathways don't delete — they fade. Under fatigue/stress, the brain reaches for the old pathway because it's "easier" (more practiced)
- Without active self-monitoring, drift is invisible to the speaker (others may notice first)
- New contexts (new job, new relationship, new city) introduce new vocal demands that may not be matched to the trained voice

### How to detect drift
1. **Monthly recording check:** record yourself reading a standard passage; compare to month-1 baseline
2. **Trusted friend feedback:** ask someone who knew your trained voice to flag if it's drifting
3. **Self-monitoring cues:** are you still feeling the buzz in your face? Or is it back in your chest?
4. **Stranger reactions:** if you start getting misgendered on the phone after months of not, drift is likely

### How to correct drift
[VERIFIED — same techniques as initial training work for refresh]
- 1-week intensive: 15 min/day of full Phase 3+ practice usually restores the trained voice
- Re-run the B7 diagnostic kit
- Re-engage with a single quality at a time if multiple have drifted
- The voice comes back FAST (days to weeks, not months)

## SECTION 3 — ILLNESS & VOICE RECOVERY

[VERIFIED — voice therapy clinical consensus]

### 3.1 The common cold / upper respiratory infection
- Nasal congestion → mouth breathing → dries vocal folds
- Coughing → irritates folds (B6 §9)
- Throat clearing → irritates folds (B6 §6)
- Post-nasal drip → throat irritation
- Voice often drops pitch during illness

**During the cold:**
- Rest the voice as much as possible
- Hydrate heavily
- Steam inhalation 2-3x/day
- Don't try to maintain the trained voice — let it rest
- Use text-to-speech if needed

**Recovery (1-2 weeks after cold resolves):**
- Don't push — folds are still recovering
- 5-10 min daily gentle practice (humming, lip trills, easy sighs)
- The trained voice usually returns within 1-2 weeks of consistent gentle practice
- If voice doesn't return after 3 weeks post-cold, see ENT

### 3.2 Laryngitis (acute)
- Voice disappears or becomes whisper
- Inflammation of the vocal folds
- Usually viral (with cold/flu); sometimes bacterial

**During laryngitis:**
- ABSOLUTE voice rest (no speaking, no whispering, no throat clearing)
- Hydrate heavily
- Steam
- Rest
- Don't try to phonate at all

**Recovery:**
- After voice returns (often 3-7 days), 1 week of gentle practice only
- The trained voice returns within 1-2 weeks of consistent gentle practice
- If hoarseness persists >2 weeks, see ENT

### 3.3 Flu / COVID-19 / severe respiratory illness
- More severe inflammation; longer recovery
- COVID specifically can cause persistent voice issues ("COVID voice" — raspy, fatigued)
- Intubation (if hospitalized) can cause vocal fold trauma

**Recovery:**
- Longer timeline (3-6 weeks for voice to fully recover)
- Gentler practice progression
- May need ENT evaluation if persistent
- The trained voice may need to be re-built gradually

### 3.4 Throat infections (strep, tonsillitis)
- Antibiotics as prescribed
- Voice rest during infection
- Recovery similar to cold

### 3.5 Allergy flares
- Post-nasal drip, throat clearing, fold irritation
- Manage allergies with medication
- Voice work can continue but with hydration emphasis
- Reduce breathy edge work during flares (irritates)

### 3.6 The universal recovery protocol
1. **Voice rest during acute illness** (no practice)
2. **Hydration + steam during illness and recovery**
3. **Gentle re-entry: 5-10 min/day for week 1 of recovery** (Phase 1 body work only)
4. **Build back: 10-15 min/day for week 2** (add Phase 2-3 quality refresh)
5. **Full practice: week 3+** (Phase 4-5 work)
6. **If voice doesn't return, see ENT**

## SECTION 4 — LIFE-GAP RECOVERY

[VERIFIED — motor learning; PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION for specific situations]

### What "life gap" means
Any period of weeks or months where the learner stops voice practice due to life circumstances:
- Major stress (job loss, divorce, death of loved one)
- Depression episode
- Major move (new city, new country)
- New demanding job
- Caregiving demands
- Hospitalization for unrelated issues
- Recovery from gender-affirming surgeries (which often involve intubation)
- Imprisonment
- Homelessness or housing instability

### What happens during the gap
- The trained voice degrades at varying rates depending on how consolidated it was
- A voice trained for 2+ years before the gap holds better than one trained for 6 months
- Old habits resurface; new habits fade
- The learner may feel like she's "lost everything"

### Recovery principles
1. **The voice is NOT lost.** Motor memory persists for years. Re-acquisition is fast.
2. **Start small.** 5 min/day of body work; build up gradually.
3. **Be kind to yourself.** Shame about "losing" the voice makes it harder to recover (anxiety = throat tension).
4. **Re-run the diagnostic kit** (B7). See where you actually are. Often better than you feared.
5. **Re-engage the foundations** (B4 body, B7 §1) before trying advanced qualities.
6. **Typical recovery timeline:** 2-4 weeks for a 6-month gap; 4-8 weeks for a 1-year gap; 2-3 months for multi-year gaps.

### The "returning to voice" curriculum
For learners returning after a gap:
1. Week 1: Phase 1 body work only (10 min/day)
2. Week 2: Add Phase 2 quality refresh (15 min/day)
3. Week 3: Add Phase 3 integration (20 min/day)
4. Week 4: Resume Phase 4 carryover (25 min/day)
5. Week 5+: Resume Phase 5 maintenance

This is essentially a fast-track version of B12's curriculum. The learner is NOT starting from zero — she's re-mapping existing (if faded) neural pathways.

## SECTION 5 — SURGERY (NON-VOICE) AND INTUBATION

[VERIFIED — anesthesiology + voice therapy consensus]

### How intubation affects voice
General anesthesia often requires intubation (a tube in the throat to maintain airway). The tube can:
- Compress vocal folds → bruising
- Cause granulomas at the contact points (intubation granulomas)
- Cause temporary swelling
- Trigger reflux

Most effects resolve in 2-6 weeks. Some persist longer.

### Pre-surgery (non-voice) considerations
- Tell the anesthesiologist you're a voice user (some use smaller tubes; some use LMA instead of intubation when possible)
- For gender-affirming surgeries specifically: tell the surgical team you're a voice user; they may be able to use LMA or smaller tube

### Post-surgery voice recovery
1. Voice rest 24-48 hours post-extubation (gentle; soft voice OK after 24h)
2. Hydrate heavily
3. Steam 2-3x/day
4. Avoid throat-clearing
5. Gentle voice re-entry over 1-2 weeks
6. The trained voice typically returns within 2-4 weeks of consistent gentle practice
7. If hoarseness persists >4 weeks post-surgery, see ENT (possible intubation granuloma)

### Gender-affirming surgery considerations
- Bottom surgery, top surgery, facial surgery — typically don't involve the airway directly
- However, general anesthesia for these often involves intubation
- Recovery from gender-affirming surgery is already emotionally taxing; voice work can wait until the body is healing

## SECTION 6 — AGING AND THE TRAINED VOICE

[VERIFIED — gerontology + voice research]

### How the voice changes with age
- **Pitch drops slightly** (often 5-15 Hz) in cis women during/after menopause due to hormonal changes
- **Vocal fold atrophy** (thinner folds) starts around age 60
- **Breath capacity decreases** with age
- **Vocal stamina decreases**
- **Presbyphonia** (age-related voice changes): breathiness, weakness, fatigue

### What this means for trans women
- If you trained at age 30, your voice at 60 will need re-tuning
- HRT changes (menopause, dose adjustments) may affect the voice
- Periodic re-evaluation (every 5 years) is wise

### Age-adapted maintenance
- Continue daily body work (breath, posture) — these matter MORE with age
- The forward-buzz and lighter-voice work may need to be re-emphasized as chest voice tries to creep back
- For singers: range contracts with age; transpose repertoire
- For older trans women: don't fight the age; find the age-appropriate feminine voice

## SECTION 7 — HRT CHANGES AND THE VOICE

[VERIFIED — endocrinology + voice research]

### Estrogen does NOT reverse testosterone-driven voice changes
Testosterone thickens vocal folds and lowers pitch irreversibly (after puberty). Estrogen does not undo this. (B6 fact.)

### But estrogen MAY affect:
- **Vocal fold hydration** — some trans women report moister folds on estrogen
- **Mucous membrane changes** — mouth/throat feel different
- **Muscle tone** — slight changes possible
- **Larynx position** — minor changes reported but not well-studied

### HRT dose changes
- Changing dose (up or down) may temporarily affect voice
- Monitor for 2-3 months after any dose change
- Some trans women report voice destabilization during dose transitions
- The trained voice usually re-stabilizes after the body adjusts

### Menopause (for trans women on estrogen)
- If estrogen is reduced (intentionally or via supply issues), some voice changes may occur
- Usually pitch drops slightly
- Maintenance practice becomes MORE important during hormonal transitions

### Pregnancy (rare for trans women but possible for some)
- Hormonal surge affects voice (pitch drops, fold swelling)
- Voice work paused during pregnancy, resumed postpartum

## SECTION 8 — THE YEAR 2+ MAINTENANCE PROTOCOL

[VERIFIED motor learning + PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION for specific structure]

### Daily micro-practice (5 min)
For year 2+ learners who have stable voice:
1. Body scan (1 min)
2. 5 belly breaths (1 min)
3. Hum with forward buzz (1 min)
4. 3 sighs in trained voice (1 min)
5. One short sentence in trained voice (1 min)

This 5-min daily practice is enough for many year 2+ learners to maintain.

### Weekly tune-up (15 min, 2-3x/week)
1. Daily micro-practice
2. Full B6 warmup sequence (5 min)
3. One quality drill from Phase 2/3 (5 min)
4. Reading aloud in trained voice (3 min)
5. Recording review (2 min)

### Monthly assessment (30 min, 1x/month)
1. Record yourself reading a standard passage
2. Compare to month-1 baseline and 6-month-ago recording
3. Re-run the diagnostic kit (B7)
4. Note any drift; if drift, do a 1-week intensive

### Annual re-evaluation (1x/year)
1. Comprehensive self-assessment
2. Optional: session with a gender-affirming SLP
3. Adjust target if life goals have shifted
4. Update the personal target voice library (B15)

## SECTION 9 — THE PSYCHOLOGY OF LONG-TERM VOICE

[PEDAGOGICAL CONVENTION — community observation]

### The "is this still me?" question
At year 2+, many trans women report a strange phase: the trained voice no longer feels like a "trained voice" — it just feels like their voice. This is the goal! But it can be disorienting.

### The voice grief cycle
Some trans women experience grief at various points:
- "I had to work so hard for what cis women get for free"
- "I'll never sound like I did before transition"
- "I miss my old singing voice" (especially for singers, per B9)
This is real and deserves acknowledgment.

### Voice neutrality
Eventually, the voice becomes neutral — neither exciting nor distressing. This is a sign of integration.

### Community connection
Year 2+ trans women often become mentors to newer transitioners. This is valuable for both.

## SECTION 10 — APP DESIGN IMPLICATIONS

### The "maintenance mode" feature
After Phase 5 (B12) is complete, the app should shift to maintenance mode:
- Daily 5-min micro-practice prompt
- Weekly tune-up reminder
- Monthly assessment with drift detection
- Annual re-evaluation prompt

### Drift detection
- Monthly recording analyzed for pitch/formant changes
- If significant drift detected, surface "Refresh Week" recommendation
- The 1-week intensive curriculum (Section 4 of this branch)

### Life-gap mode
- Detect when learner hasn't practiced in 2+ weeks
- Offer gentle re-entry curriculum (5 min/day building up)
- No streak-shaming; reset the streak counter; meet the learner where she is

### Illness mode
- Learner flags illness; app pauses regular curriculum
- Offers recovery protocol (Section 3)
- Tracks time since illness; recommends ENT visit if voice doesn't return

### Long-term community
- Connect year-2+ learners with each other (peer mentorship)
- Optional: connect with newer transitioners as mentors
- Celebrate long-term milestones (1 year, 2 years, 5 years)

### Age/hormone awareness
- Periodic check-ins: "Has your HRT changed?" "Are you going through menopause?"
- Adjust curriculum if relevant
- Provide age-appropriate content for older learners
